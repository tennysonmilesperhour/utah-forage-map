# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Mushroom foragers worldwide, from first-season learners to experienced field naturalists. They use the product on a phone while checking current field signals or recording a find, and on a larger screen while planning trips, comparing habitats, and reviewing community knowledge.

## Product Purpose

Mushroom Forage Map helps people find recent, reviewed mushroom observations anywhere in the world, understand found-date and habitat patterns, and contribute useful field reports. The public map must deliver value without an account. An account earns its place by unlocking a private logbook, saved places, submissions, and community participation.

## Brand Personality

Practical, welcoming, trustworthy. The product should feel like a well-kept field desk: knowledgeable without being academic, outdoors-aware without becoming rustic, and calm enough to use while making a real decision.

## Anti-references

- Account walls that block the public map before users understand its value.
- Generic SaaS landing pages, oversized marketing headlines, and decorative feature-card grids.
- Dense directory pages that make every link compete at the same visual weight.
- Brown or beige outdoor palettes, novelty wilderness motifs, and decorative map styling that obscures data.
- Tiny, low-contrast controls that fail in bright outdoor light.

## Design Principles

1. Map before membership. Public observations and filters are immediately usable.
2. Earn every account prompt. Ask users to sign in only when they save, submit, or build a logbook.
3. Make trust visible. Show review state, source, safety guidance, and privacy expectations where decisions happen.
4. Work at trailhead speed. Keep common actions obvious, compact, and comfortable on a phone.
5. Protect the place and the person. Avoid exposing sensitive locations unnecessarily and never imply that map data alone confirms edibility.
6. Let geography lead. Search, map bounds, found dates, and observed evidence adapt to the user's region instead of assuming one climate or hemisphere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, focus visibility, semantics, and keyboard access. Keep interactive targets at least 44 by 44 pixels on touch screens. Do not use color as the only indicator for edibility, verification, source, or error state. Respect reduced-motion preferences and write for mixed experience levels without patronizing beginners.
