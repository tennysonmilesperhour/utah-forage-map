## Identification

Jack-o'-lanterns are orange from cap to stem and usually grow as tight clusters from stumps, roots, or buried wood. The crowded gills are thin, sharp-edged blades that run down the stem. Cut flesh remains orange rather than exposing the pale, fibrous interior expected in a chanterelle.

Some gills can emit a faint green light in complete darkness after eyes adjust, but many specimens do not glow visibly. Bioluminescence is an interesting confirmation when present, never a required test.

## Why it matters

Eating jack-o'-lanterns can cause severe cramps, vomiting, and diarrhea. The eastern North American *O. illudens* has western, European, and other regional relatives that share the same basic orange, clustered, gilled pattern.

The fungus decomposes buried hardwood, so a cluster may appear to grow from lawn soil even when roots or a stump remain underneath.

## Memorable fact

The light is produced in the gills by a chemical reaction involving luciferin and oxygen, similar in principle to other bioluminescent organisms.
