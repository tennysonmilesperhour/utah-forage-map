## Identification

Gem-studded puffballs are white, golf-ball to pear shaped, and covered with tiny detachable cone spines. When the spines rub away they leave a net-like pattern. A vertical slice must show a uniform, pure-white interior with no internal outline or differentiated parts.

That slice test is not optional. A deadly Amanita enclosed in its universal veil can resemble a young puffball from the outside.

## Uses and ecology

Only fresh, uniformly white specimens are considered edible, and they should be cooked. Older puffballs form a powdery spore mass. Deliberately inhaling that cloud can cause lycoperdonosis, a rare lung inflammation.

## Memorable fact

The name *Lycoperdon* means "wolf fart," and mature puffballs use raindrop impacts to pump spores into the air.
