## Identification

The caps are thin, pure white, and fan- or ear-shaped, with no stem or only a stub. Gills are narrow and crowded. It grows in overlapping clusters on rotting conifer wood.

## Why it matters

Angel wings were eaten in Japan and North America until a 2004 outbreak of acute encephalopathy in Japan killed several people, most with kidney disease. It is no longer considered safe.

## Memorable fact

It looks so much like a delicate oyster mushroom that it was widely eaten for generations.
