## Identification

Meadow mushrooms have smooth white to cream caps, short stems with a thin ring, and gills that are free from the stem. Young gills are distinctly pink, then darken through brown to chocolate as the spores mature. The flesh is white and should smell mild and mushroom-like.

Excavate the base. There must be no cup, sack, or other volva. Check the youngest and oldest specimens in a group because gill color changes with age. Strong yellow staining or an inky, antiseptic odor points away from this species.

## Uses and ecology

Correctly identified meadow mushrooms are cooked edibles, but lawn habitat exposes them to herbicides, pet waste, road runoff, and a dangerous cast of lookalikes. Avoid treated or contaminated ground. The genus *Agaricus* is difficult, and local expert confirmation is appropriate before any culinary use.

Fairy rings form as underground mycelium expands outward and fruits near its active edge.

## Memorable fact

The store-bought button mushroom is a close relative, but resemblance to a cultivated mushroom is never enough to identify a wild one.
