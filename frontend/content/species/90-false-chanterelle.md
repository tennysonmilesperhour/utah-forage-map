## Identification

The cap is orange, soft, and felty, often darker in the center. The gills are thin, crowded, bright orange, and fork many times. The flesh is thin and soft rather than solid and stringy. It grows on conifer litter or rotting wood.

## Why it matters

False chanterelle is not seriously poisonous, but it causes gastrointestinal upset in some people. Learning to separate its true gills from a chanterelle's blunt ridges is basic training for chanterelle hunters.

## Memorable fact

Its gills fork so often that the underside can look like a net of orange veins.
