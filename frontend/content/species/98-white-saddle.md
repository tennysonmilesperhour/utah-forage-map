## Identification

The cap is cream to white, thin, and folded into two or three saddle-like lobes. The stem is white and deeply furrowed with sharp ribs and hollow chambers. It grows from soil in woods.

## Why it matters

Helvella were once eaten after cooking, but they are toxic raw and some contain compounds related to gyromitrin. Most modern guides recommend against eating them.

## Memorable fact

The ribbed stem looks like it has been sculpted, which makes it one of the most photogenic woodland fungi.
