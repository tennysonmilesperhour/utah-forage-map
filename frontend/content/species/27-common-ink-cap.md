## Identification

Common ink caps form dense clusters of smooth gray to gray-brown bell-shaped caps, often from buried wood. Fine radial grooves become more visible toward the margin. The crowded gills begin white, turn gray, then black as the cap edge curls and dissolves into inky liquid.

Unlike shaggy mane, the cap lacks prominent white shaggy scales. Clustering and a shared buried wood source are useful, but the ink-cap group still contains many similar species.

## Why it matters

This species contains coprine. When alcohol is consumed from roughly several hours before to as long as a few days after the mushroom, acetaldehyde can accumulate and cause flushing, headache, nausea, palpitations, and low blood pressure. Reactions are usually self-limited but can require medical care.

Some field guides describe carefully prepared specimens as edible when alcohol is strictly avoided. Because timing and individual risk are hard to control, this guide does not place it in the ordinary edible category.

## Memorable fact

The former genus name *Coprinus* comes from a word for dung, while *atramentaria* refers to black writing ink.
