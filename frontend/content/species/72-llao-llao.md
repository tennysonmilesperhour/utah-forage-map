## Identification

Llao llao forms golf-ball-sized, orange to cream spheres covered in shallow pits, attached to woody galls on southern beech branches. The flesh is gelatinous and sweet. It fruits in spring.

## Uses and ecology

Cyttaria are parasites of Nothofagus trees, causing the woody galls. Llao llao has been eaten by Indigenous peoples of Patagonia, including the Mapuche, and is used in fermented drinks.

## Memorable fact

Charles Darwin collected a related species in Tierra del Fuego during the Beagle voyage, and it was later named Cyttaria darwinii.
