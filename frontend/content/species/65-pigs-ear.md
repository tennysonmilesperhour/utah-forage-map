## Identification

Young fruiting bodies are purple clubs that widen into flat-topped, lopsided vases with tan to lilac tops. The underside is covered in wrinkled, vein-like violet ridges running down the stem. Several often fuse at the base. The flesh is firm and white.

## Uses and ecology

Pig's ear depends on old, undisturbed conifer forest and has declined in much of Europe. Where it is common, as in parts of western North America, harvest lightly. Where it is listed, leave it.

## Memorable fact

It is one of the few mushrooms on the European Red List of threatened fungi that is also a traditional edible.
