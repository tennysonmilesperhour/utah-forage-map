## Identification

The cap is tan to brown. Pores start white and become pinkish with age. The stem carries a coarse dark brown net. The flesh is intensely bitter, which is why one specimen can ruin a pan of porcini, but tasting is not a safe identification method: rely on the pinkish pores and the dark net.

## Why it matters

Bitter bolete is the most common reason porcini collections get ruined. It is mycorrhizal with conifers and hardwoods across Europe and eastern North America.

## Memorable fact

The species name felleus means bile-like, after its bitterness.
