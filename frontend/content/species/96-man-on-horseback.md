## Identification

The cap is yellow with an olive to brownish center, often gritty with sand. Gills are sulphur yellow and notched where they meet the stem. The stem is yellow. It grows in sandy pine forests.

## Why it matters

This mushroom was traditionally eaten in Europe until case series in France and Poland linked repeated meals to rhabdomyolysis, a breakdown of muscle tissue that can damage the heart and kidneys. Several countries now warn against it.

## Memorable fact

It was sold in markets for generations before its toxicity was recognized in the early 2000s.
