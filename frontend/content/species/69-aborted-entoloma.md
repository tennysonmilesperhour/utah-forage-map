## Identification

Two forms grow together. The gilled form has a gray cap and gills that turn pink, dropping pink spores. Nearby are white to pinkish, irregular lumps with a marbled interior. These lumps form when the fungus and honey mushrooms interact.

## Uses and ecology

Recent research suggests Entoloma parasitizes honey mushroom fruiting bodies to make the lumps. The lumps are firm and eaten by experienced foragers.

## Memorable fact

For years the lumps were thought to be deformed Entoloma; studies now indicate they are mostly altered honey mushrooms.
