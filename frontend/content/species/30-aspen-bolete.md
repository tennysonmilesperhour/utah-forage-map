## Identification

The cap is orange to orange-brown and sits above a thick pale stem dotted with rough dark flecks called scabers. The underside has pores that begin whitish and age gray to brownish. Cut flesh may slowly discolor gray, violet, or dark near the stem base.

Find and photograph the nearby aspen. *Leccinum* species are tightly associated with host trees, and an orange cap without host context is not a reliable species identification.

## Uses and risk

Aspen boletes have a history of culinary use, but western *Leccinum* collections have also been associated with significant gastrointestinal illness. Misidentification, inadequate cooking, and individual sensitivity are difficult to separate in reports. This guide therefore treats the species as edible only with expert-level caution rather than as a routine food mushroom.

The fungus forms a mycorrhizal partnership with aspen roots and often appears in groups after summer rain.

## Memorable fact

Scabers look like dark scales, but they are clusters of projecting cells that give the group its scaber-stalk name.
