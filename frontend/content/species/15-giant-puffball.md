## Identification

Giant puffballs are round to irregular white fruiting bodies without a cap, gills, or conventional stem. Mature specimens may exceed 30 centimeters across. The surface begins smooth and white, then yellows or browns as the interior transforms into spores.

Cut from top to bottom before doing anything else. Fresh interior tissue must be solid, homogeneous, and pure white. A yellow, olive, brown, watery, or powdery center is past the edible stage. Any internal mushroom-shaped structure rules out a puffball.

## Uses and ecology

Fresh, correctly identified white flesh is sliced and cooked. Giant size does not replace the cut test, and smaller specimens demand even more care. They are saprotrophic, obtaining nutrients from organic matter in soil rather than partnering with a living tree.

Old specimens release enormous clouds of microscopic spores. Avoid deliberately kicking or inhaling them because concentrated puffball spores can inflame the lungs.

## Memorable fact

A single mature giant puffball can produce trillions of spores.
