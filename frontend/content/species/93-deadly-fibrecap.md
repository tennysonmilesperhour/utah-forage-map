## Identification

The cap is conical to bell-shaped and cream to white, with radiating fibers and a margin that often splits. With age or bruising, cap, gills, and stem turn brick red. The spore print is dull brown.

## Why it matters

Muscarine poisoning causes sweating, salivation, tears, and a dangerously slow heart rate. Deaths have followed meals of this species, usually after confusion with spring edibles.

## Memorable fact

It fruits in the same weeks and places as St George's mushroom, which is why it is often called its deadly double.
