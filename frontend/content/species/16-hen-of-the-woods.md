## Identification

Hen of the woods forms one branching cluster that can weigh many kilograms. Dozens of gray, brown, and cream fan-shaped caps arise from a thick shared base. The undersides are white and finely porous, and the pores continue down the branches. Fresh fronds are flexible with white, firm flesh.

Check for staining. A lookalike that darkens dramatically where handled points toward black-staining polypore, not maitake. Also inspect the tree association and attachment rather than identifying a detached market-style cluster.

## Uses and ecology

Young outer fronds are valued when cooked; the dense central base and old edges become tough. Maitake is also cultivated. Supplement extracts are a separate product from a food identification, and health claims should not replace clinical care.

The fungus causes a root and butt rot in hardwoods. Its fruiting bodies may return near the same tree for many autumns, sometimes before the weakened root system is obvious.

## Memorable fact

The Japanese name maitake is commonly translated as dancing mushroom, often linked to the delight of finding a valuable cluster.
