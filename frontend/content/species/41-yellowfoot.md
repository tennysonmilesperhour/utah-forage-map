## Identification

Yellowfoot has a thin brown cap, often perforated in the center, above a hollow, flattened yellow to orange stem. The underside has gray to yellowish, blunt, forked ridges rather than true gills. It grows in large troops in moss and rotting wood. Break the stem: it is hollow.

## Uses and ecology

Yellowfoot fruits after most other mushrooms have finished, making it a late-season favorite in Europe and the Pacific Northwest. It dries well. It is mycorrhizal with conifers in wet forests.

## Memorable fact

Fruiting can continue under light snow, which is why many foragers call it the winter chanterelle.
