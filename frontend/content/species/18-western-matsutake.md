## Identification

Western matsutake begins partly buried, often lifting a cap of duff as it expands. The cap is white beneath pressed brown fibers or scales. The stem is dense and solid, with a cottony sheathing ring and firm tapered base but no sack-like volva. White gills are crowded and may notch near the stem.

Its odor is often described as spicy, cinnamon-like, resinous, or like dirty socks. Treat that as supporting evidence only. Dig the complete base, photograph the habitat, and confirm every structural feature.

## Uses and ecology

This is a commercially harvested mushroom with deep importance in Japanese cuisine, where aroma and firm texture are central. It should be cooked, and rare gastrointestinal reactions are documented. The much larger risk is mistaken identity with kidney-toxic *Amanita smithiana* in the Pacific Northwest.

Western matsutake is mycorrhizal and connected to living host roots. Harvest and permit rules vary sharply by forest and jurisdiction.

## Memorable fact

North America's western species received its current name after genetic work separated it from the Eurasian matsutake and eastern pine mushroom.
