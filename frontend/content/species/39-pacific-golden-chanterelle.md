## Identification

The cap is orange-yellow and often wavy, with an inrolled margin when young. Underneath, the blunt ridges fork and cross-connect, and often have a faint pinkish cast. The flesh is solid, pale, and stringy when torn, with a mild fruity smell. It grows singly or scattered from soil, not in dense clusters on wood.

## Uses and ecology

This chanterelle partners with Douglas-fir and hemlock and supports a large commercial harvest in Oregon, Washington, and British Columbia. Cut rather than rake, and leave small buttons.

## Memorable fact

It is the official state mushroom of Oregon.
