## Identification

The bracket is soft, fleshy, and liver- to blood-red, shaped like a tongue or kidney. Cut it and the flesh shows red and white marbling like raw beef, releasing red juice. The cream underside is made of separate tubes that can be seen with a lens. It grows low on the trunk of oak or chestnut.

## Uses and ecology

Beefsteak fungus causes a slow brown rot that stains oak heartwood a rich brown, once valued by furniture makers. The flesh is tart from organic acids and is usually sliced thin, soaked, and cooked.

## Memorable fact

Oak stained by this fungus was sold as "brown oak" and prized for its warm color.
