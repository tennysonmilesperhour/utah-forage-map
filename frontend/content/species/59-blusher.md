## Identification

The cap is pinkish to reddish brown with scattered pale gray to pinkish warts. Gills are white. The ring is striated on its upper surface. The key feature is blushing: bruised or insect-eaten flesh, especially in the swollen stem base, turns wine red.

## Uses and ecology

Blushers are eaten in parts of Europe after cooking, but raw flesh contains compounds that damage red blood cells. Because the Amanita genus includes deadly species, experts consider it unsuitable for beginners.

## Memorable fact

Insect tunnels in the stem turn pink, so a wormy blusher often shows its identity from the inside.
