## Identification

Young caps are rounded, then expand into broad parasols with a pale background and flat tan to brown scales. The tall stem carries a thick ring that can often slide. Gills are free from the stem and begin white, becoming gray-green only as spores mature.

Make a full spore print on white and dark paper. The dull green print is the decisive field clue, but waiting for it does not make a meal candidate safe. Young specimens can have white gills and are commonly mistaken for edible parasols.

## Why it matters

This warm-climate lawn mushroom is among the most frequently ingested toxic mushrooms in the United States. Poisoning usually produces intense gastrointestinal illness, sometimes including bloody diarrhea and dehydration. Children and pets encounter it in yards as well as adults seeking edible parasols.

Remove fruiting bodies from play areas using gloves or a tool if accidental eating is a concern; touching the mushroom does not cause poisoning.

## Memorable fact

Its green spores supplied the scientific name: *molybdites* refers to a lead-green color.
