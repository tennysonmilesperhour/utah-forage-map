## Identification

Chaga is a sterile growth rather than a typical mushroom. It bursts from a birch trunk as a hard, deeply cracked black mass that looks like burnt charcoal. Break a fragment and the inside is a rusty yellow-orange cork. It is almost always on birch; the same appearance on another tree calls for caution.

## Uses and ecology

Chaga is a long-lived parasite of birch that slowly decays the heartwood. It is simmered as tea across northern Europe and Asia. Growth is slow, so harvest only a portion, never cut into the tree, and avoid trees on protected land. Case reports describe kidney injury from large daily amounts because chaga is very high in oxalate.

## Memorable fact

The true spore-bearing stage appears only after the host tree dies, as a thin crust hidden under loosened bark.
