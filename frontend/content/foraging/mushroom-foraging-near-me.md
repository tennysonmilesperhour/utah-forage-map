## How can I find mushroom information near me?
Use the [worldwide map](/map) to find recent public observations around a place, then check the relevant [regional collection](/regions) and land manager. The map opens near the country detected from your connection. Search for a city, region or country to choose a different view. Precise device location is optional.

## A useful search sequence
1. Search for a place you can realistically visit.
2. Keep the date window visible. A recent record and a years-old record answer different questions.
3. Narrow by a species or habitat only after looking at the overall coverage. Too many filters can hide useful context.
4. Open individual records and examine the source, date and review information.
5. Read the corresponding species guide and any dangerous lookalikes.
6. Check legal access, collecting rules, closures and the actual route independently of the pin.

If no records appear, widen the view or relax a filter. A blank result may reflect limited reporting rather than a mushroom-free landscape. If the country is wrong because of a VPN or network routing, a manual search takes priority over the default view.

## Why are some pins approximate?
The public map supports location privacy. An approximate pin is deliberately not an exact collecting coordinate. Use it to understand a general area, not to navigate to a presumed individual mushroom or infer a contributor’s private site. The [editorial and privacy explanation](/about) describes the distinction.

## Does a public pin mean I can collect there?
No. A biological record does not grant access or permission to remove anything. Public ownership, public access and permission to forage are separate questions. The [permissions guide](/learn/foraging/foraging-permissions) helps you collect the right information from the responsible authority. For an example of site-specific requirements, consult the [Siuslaw National Forest’s mushroom permit page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/siuslaw/permits/mushroom-permits), which applies to that forest rather than every public landscape.

## How do I avoid over-relying on the map?
Use it to plan an observation walk. Bring local references, document what you encounter, and resolve identification separately. [Beginner field skills](/learn/foraging/beginners) and [recording a find](/learn/foraging/recording-finds) are good next steps. A high record count is not an abundance estimate, an access guarantee or a food-safety endorsement.
