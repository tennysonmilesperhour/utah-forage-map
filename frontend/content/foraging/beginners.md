## Where should a beginner start?
Start with a local identification walk and a notebook. Choose one habitat near home, learn a few recognizable groups, and record what you actually see. A successful first outing can end with photographs and questions rather than a basket of food. [NAMA’s introduction to mycology](https://namyco.org/beginners-guide-to-mycology/) describes clubs, observation and field learning as useful starting points.

## A practical first-outing plan
1. Choose a place you are allowed to enter. Separately check whether collecting is allowed.
2. Open the [mushroom map](/) and use recent dates to identify areas with recorded activity. A pin is an observation, not a harvesting recommendation.
3. Read one [regional guide](/regions) and a few [species profiles](/learn). Notice what each profile says about missing evidence and lookalikes.
4. Carry a charged phone, route information, weather-appropriate clothing, water, and a way to record notes. Tell someone your plan when appropriate to the terrain.
5. Photograph organisms in place. If collection is permitted and appropriate, keep specimens for study separately from anything intended as food.
6. Review the record with a knowledgeable local person. Write down unresolved features rather than filling them in from a picture online.

## What should go in the notebook?
Record the date, a general locality, habitat, what the mushroom grows from, and the nearby trees you can actually recognize. Include a scale in photographs. Separate what you observed from your proposed identification: “pores visible underneath” is evidence; “probably a porcini” is a hypothesis. The [recording guide](/learn/foraging/recording-finds) provides a reusable checklist.

## Which mushrooms are safe for beginners?
There is no universally safe beginner list that replaces identification. Familiar common names can cover several species, and local lookalikes matter. This archive’s edibility categories organize study material; they do not approve a specimen for eating. [Oregon Poison Center](https://www.ohsu.edu/oregon-poison-center/plants-mushrooms) advises expert identification before consuming a wild mushroom.

## What should I do next?
Practice the [identification evidence checklist](/learn/foraging/mushroom-identification), compare your notes with the source material, and revisit the same place over time. If an ingestion may have occurred, use [poison-response guidance](/learn/safety) immediately rather than waiting for an online identification.
