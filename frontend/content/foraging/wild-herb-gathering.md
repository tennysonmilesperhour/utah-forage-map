## How should I start learning wild herbs?
Begin with living plants you can revisit through the season. Record their structure and setting before considering collection. A familiar name or culinary association is not enough: the precise plant, part, stage and local context matter. Kew’s [Grow Wild guidance](https://growwild.kew.org/sensory-and-edible-native-plants) favors growing known edible native plants and cautions that wild identification and over-foraging can cause problems.

## Use the atlas in a deliberate order
The [global herb atlas](/herbs/atlas) contains 44 reference profiles and six regional collections. It is a starting collection rather than an inventory of every useful or hazardous plant worldwide.

1. Search by scientific name as well as common name. Check that the page describes the plant you mean.
2. Read the field marks and compare multiple visible structures. A leaf silhouette alone is a weak match.
3. Open the toxic lookalikes before looking at uses.
4. Check the part and stage discussed. Information about one plant part should not be extended to the whole plant.
5. Follow the botanical and safety sources and check their regional scope.
6. Record unresolved questions. Do not gather for consumption when identification or safety remains uncertain.

## Compare references without turning them into a safety score
The atlas comparison view places two profiles beside each other. Use it to notice differences and missing evidence. It does not calculate identification confidence. [Poison Control’s plant guidance](https://www.poison.org/articles/plant) explains that toxicity can involve different parts of a plant and that mistaken identity can be serious.

## Plan the place and the season
Explore [herb regions](/herbs/regions) for climate context and local references, then check the [field skills guide](/herbs/fieldcraft) for contamination, sustainable gathering and preparation cautions. Permission to enter a place does not automatically include permission to remove plants. There is no single worldwide percentage or quantity that makes harvesting sustainable for every species.

## What are watch zones and the pantry for?
[The Verdant Hours](/herbs) offers a seasonal almanac, optional local weather, private watch zones and a pantry. These help organize observations and gathered material; they do not certify identity, medicinal benefit or safe preparation. The astronomical moon display is separate from botanical evidence. For concentrated products or possible medication interactions, use an appropriate health professional rather than treating a foraging profile as medical advice.
