## What is the important distinction?
“Morel” and “false morel” are common names used across groups of mushrooms, not a complete identification system. A comparison must account for the whole specimen, the relevant species and the region. [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/gathering-wild-grown-plants-and-fungi/harvesting-morel-mushrooms) emphasizes correctly identifying a morel and avoiding poisonous lookalikes.

## Questions to ask while studying a specimen
Use the [morel profile](/learn/species/morel) and [false morel profile](/learn/species/false-morel) together. The following questions organize evidence; answering them does not establish edibility.

| Feature | What to document | Why a shortcut fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cap surface | Distinct pits and ridges, folds, lobes, and the complete shape | A distant or blurred view can obscure the structure |
| Cap attachment | Where the cap connects to the stem | The underside or attachment can be hidden in a top view |
| Interior | The complete cap-to-base structure of an appropriately collected specimen | One cropped section does not show the whole mushroom |
| Local context | Region, date, habitat and applicable local species | Common names travel more easily than identification knowledge |

Keep photos linked to the same specimen. Compare descriptions in more than one expert source. If an essential feature is missing, stop the identification there rather than treating the absence of evidence as a match.

## Does correct identification settle preparation safety?
No. Identification and preparation are separate issues. A [CDC investigation of the 2023 Montana outbreak](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7310a1.htm) linked morel consumption to 51 reported illnesses and two deaths; raw consumption was more strongly associated with illness. That finding is not a cooking-time guarantee or a reason to attempt to detoxify a lookalike.

This guide does not provide a preparation method for false morels. Use a qualified local identifier for the specimen and authoritative food-safety guidance for any confirmed food mushroom. Do not taste an unknown mushroom to settle its identity.

## What if someone already ate it?
Contact a poison center promptly rather than waiting for a symptom pattern or a reply to a photograph. In the United States, call 1-800-222-1222. Outside the US, use the local poison service or emergency number. Follow the [poison-response page](/learn/safety); Oregon’s [plants and mushrooms guidance](https://www.ohsu.edu/oregon-poison-center/plants-mushrooms) also advises calling immediately when a potentially poisonous mushroom has been ingested.
