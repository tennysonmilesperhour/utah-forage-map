## Is there one mushroom season?
No single worldwide calendar describes all mushroom fruiting. A month that is useful in one region may be irrelevant in another. Species, local climate, elevation and habitat all affect what a field visit might encounter. Treat a broad seasonal label as a starting question and check local evidence before planning a trip.

## Three time scales to compare
The site offers several views that answer different questions:

| View | Question it helps answer | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recent map records | What has someone reported in the selected area recently? | That it is still there or accessible |
| Regional 14- and 90-day summaries | How much recent public recording is visible? | Actual population size or equal sampling effort |
| All-time monthly charts | In which months have records accumulated? | A forecast for this year or this weekend |

Open the [regional collections](/regions), select a habitat region, and compare its recent summary with its longer-term chart. Then use the map to look at the dates of individual records. Read the chart’s source and sample size; a tall bar made from sparse or uneven observations deserves caution.

## Why a blank map is not proof of absence
People do not visit every forest equally or upload every organism they see. Records also depend on identification and review. iNaturalist’s [Data Quality Assessment](https://help.inaturalist.org/en/support/solutions/articles/151000169936) describes how observations qualify for different quality grades. Those grades concern the record; they do not turn community sampling into a complete biological survey.

## How should weather fit into the plan?
Use a current local forecast for travel conditions and compare your own notes across visits. Avoid treating a single rain event or temperature threshold as a universal fruiting rule. The useful question is whether your selected species, habitat and region have a consistent local pattern, and what evidence supports it.

## Mushrooms and herbs need different calendars
A fungal fruiting body and a plant’s usable leaf, flower, seed or fruit are different stages. The [herb atlas](/herbs/atlas) organizes plant references with stage and regional context; its calendar is not a mushroom forecast. For morels, read the [morel profile](/learn/species/morel) and [University of Minnesota Extension’s regional guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/gathering-wild-grown-plants-and-fungi/harvesting-morel-mushrooms), recognizing its geographic scope.
