## Can a photograph identify an edible mushroom?
A photograph can support a discussion, but it cannot establish that an unknown mushroom is safe to eat. Missing views, developmental stage, regional species and damaged structures can all limit an identification. Norway’s [Poisons Information Centre](https://www.helsenorge.no/en/poison-information/poisonous-mushrooms) explicitly advises against using AI to identify mushrooms for eating.

## Build an evidence record
Use this checklist to organize observations for a field guide or qualified local identifier. It is a documentation checklist, not an identification key.

| Evidence | Record what is visible | Common gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In-place view | Growth pattern, surrounding ground or wood, nearby vegetation | A cropped image loses the habitat |
| Cap | Surface, shape, color, size and condition | Color alone is not distinctive |
| Underside | Gills, pores, teeth or another structure | A top-down photo hides this entirely |
| Stem and base | Whole stem, attachment and any remaining structures | A cut stem can omit important features |
| Interior | Relevant internal structure, only when collection and examination are appropriate | Assuming a photograph shows the whole organism |
| Context | Date, locality, substrate and several growth stages if present | Applying a distant region’s guide uncritically |

Keep photographs of a single specimen together. Do not combine the cap of one mushroom with the underside of another similar-looking mushroom. Leave uncertain observations labeled as uncertain.

## Use the field profiles as comparisons
The [fungi library](/) separates field marks, lookalikes, habitat, source links and public observation signals. Start with the underlying structures, not the color of the card or an edibility badge. Follow cited references and check whether they describe your region. Some groups require microscopy or other specialist evidence beyond what this website contains.

For a concrete study pair, compare the [morel](/learn/species/morel) and [false morel](/learn/species/false-morel) profiles with the [morel lookalike discussion](/learn/foraging/morel-lookalikes). Matching a few listed features is not a food-safety decision.

## How should I ask for help?
Share the original images, date, general region and observed habitat. State what is unknown. NAMA’s [scientific collection guidance](https://namyco.org/publications/mcilvainea-journal-of-american-amateur-mycology/making-scientific-vouchers-of-north-american-macrofungi/) explains why field notes and linked specimen records matter. If the question follows an ingestion, contact a poison service first; an identification forum is not emergency care.
